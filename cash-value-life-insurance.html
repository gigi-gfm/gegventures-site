How the money works

One policy, three financial jobs

Every premium you pay is split between protection and a growing cash account you control.

1

It grows tax-deferred

Cash value compounds inside the policy without a yearly tax bill. Whole life credits a steady guaranteed rate plus potential dividends; indexed universal life ties growth to a market index with a floor that limits downside.

2

You can borrow it back

Once cash value builds, you can take policy loans — often without credit checks or the age-59½ penalty that applies to a 401(k) or IRA. Use it for a down payment, a business, or an emergency, and pay it back on your terms.

3

It pays out tax-free

Whatever you don’t use passes to your beneficiaries as an income-tax-free death benefit — a legacy that arrives exactly when your family needs it most.

Straight answers

Common questions

Isn’t “buy term and invest the difference” better?

For many people, yes — and we’ll tell you if that’s you. Cash value life insurance shines when you’ve already filled your tax-advantaged accounts, want permanent coverage, or need guarantees and liquidity that market accounts can’t offer.

How soon can I use the cash value?

Cash value builds slowly in the early years because of upfront costs, then accelerates. It’s a long-term tool — typically meaningful after year five to ten, not a short-term savings account.

Are the returns guaranteed?

Whole life includes a guaranteed minimum; dividends are not guaranteed. Indexed universal life has a floor but variable, cap-limited upside. We’ll show you the guaranteed column and a realistic column — never just the best-case illustration.
